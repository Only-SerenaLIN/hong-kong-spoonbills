# Process

## Tools

I used the GBIF website to filter and download occurrence records for *Platalea minor* in Hong Kong. I used ChatGPT/Codex to inspect the archive and its fields, adapt the template code, and diagnose syntax and indentation errors. I ran both scripts in VS Code and checked the printed record counts and the resulting picture.

## Kept

I first kept twelve monthly maps, but then replaced them with a circular migration calendar because the annual arrival, winter peak and departure became easier to read as one continuous cycle. I kept exact monthly counts beside the circle and used a square-root scale so that the quiet summer months remain visible. A pale-to-deep teal gradient reinforces the same monthly change without introducing another variable. I also kept only the six complete years from 2020 to 2025.

## Rejected

I rejected using thousands of bird icons, because one GBIF row is an occurrence record rather than one individual bird. I used one silhouette per month and encoded the count through its position instead. I also rejected interpreting the number of GBIF records as the number of birds. Recording effort varies, repeated visits can produce multiple records, and some places are observed more often than others.
